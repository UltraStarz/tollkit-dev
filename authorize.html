Give an agent an allowance

Today, letting an AI agent pay for something means putting a wallet's private key in a config file. That key can spend everything in the wallet, forever, anywhere. This is the other way: the agent gets its own key, capped — a set amount per week, expiring on a date you pick, revocable from here.

Works with any service that takes x402. We built it because we run some, but the allowance is between you, your wallet and your agent — Tollkit is not in the middle of it and cannot spend it.

Free to create, and nothing on what your agent spends — not a percentage, not a markup, not ever. That is not generosity: the allowance is between your wallet and your agent, so we are not in that path and could not take a cut if we wanted to. We make money when you choose to spend some of it with us.

Preview. Nothing here touches a wallet or the chain yet — connecting and signing are simulated so the flow can be reviewed before the rails are wired in. Do not use any value shown here for real money.

2 · How much it may spend

The allowance refills at the start of each period and does not accumulate — an unused week does not become a bigger next week.

An allowance you forget about is the one that costs you. An expiry is a dead man's switch.

3 · The agent's own key

Generated in your browser, right now. It is never sent anywhere — not to us, not to the chain. We could not produce a copy of it if you asked.

A cap limits what you can lose. It does not stop a mistake.

An agent that reads web pages can be given instructions by a web page. In May 2026 an agent on Base was talked into sending roughly $150,000 by a message hidden in Morse code — and the attacker escalated its permissions first, so the transfer limits never applied at all. Princeton researchers have since shown that guardrails written into an agent's prompt can be defeated by poisoning what it remembers, with transactions on mainnet to prove it.

Because this allowance can be spent at any x402 service, a stolen agent key can spend the full cap on something you would never have bought. That is the trade for not restricting where it works.

We are not going to tell you a cap makes your agent safe. It makes your exposure a number you picked in advance. Set it to what you would be annoyed to lose in a week, not what you plan to spend. You can always raise it later.

5 · Point your agent at it

Your agent signs its own payments with this key, like any other x402 buyer. Nobody pulls from your wallet — not us, not the service being paid.

Straight answers

    

      
Who holds the money
You do, in your own wallet, the whole time.

      
Who holds the agent key
You do. It is generated in your browser and never transmitted.

      
What Tollkit charges
Nothing to create it, and nothing on what your agent spends. We charge per call, only at our own services, only if you use them.

      
What Tollkit can do
Nothing else. We are not a party to the allowance and cannot spend it.

      
What the agent can do
Spend up to the cap, per period, until the expiry or until you revoke.

      
What the agent cannot do
Touch anything else in your wallet, or exceed the cap.

      
Where it lives
On Base, as a signed permission — not a row in our database.

      
If Tollkit disappears
Nothing changes. Revoking does not need us, and the allowance expires on its own.

      
Is this still a key in a file
Yes — but one that can only ever cost you the cap. That is the whole point.
