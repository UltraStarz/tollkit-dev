Know the moment your endpoint breaks

Weigh Station checks your endpoint every 15 minutes for 30 days and sends a signed webhook when something changes. $5 per endpoint, paid in USDC over x402. Nothing renews by itself.

DownAfter two failed checks in a row. Once, not every 15 minutes.
Back upWhen it recovers.
Wrong walletIf your quote starts asking buyers to pay a wallet that isn’t yours.
Price changedWhen the amount you quote changes.
ExpiringThree days before your 30 days end.
Public recordState and 30-day uptime anyone can look up. Never your webhook.
  1. Prepare (free). We check your endpoint and read the wallet it asks buyers to pay. 
    curl -s -X POST https://extract.tollkit.dev/weigh/prepare \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"url":"https://your-api.com/endpoint","webhookUrl":"https://your-app.com/hooks/weigh"}'
  2. Sign the returned message with that same wallet. It’s free and moves no money, and it’s how we know the endpoint is yours.
  3. Pay $5 by POSTing the same fields plus signature to /weigh/subscribe with any x402 client. A bad signature or a wallet mismatch is refused and never charged.

Paying for monitoring never changes what Weigh Station’s checks report about your service, or where it ranks. Look up any endpoint: GET https://extract.tollkit.dev/weigh/status?url=…

What these tools promise

Every line here is a branch in the code with a test behind it — not marketing. They are also the things a general-purpose “read any page” service structurally cannot promise, which is the reason to point an agent at these instead.

It won’t answer about the wrong product

Dead product URLs redirect to category pages all the time. A generic reader extracts whatever it lands on and hands you a real price for a different item — and nothing about the answer looks wrong. We refuse instead, and don’t charge. Pass allow_redirect if you want whatever the URL resolves to.

Failures are free

Every failure path returns an error status, and x402 settles nothing on an error. Blocked sites, dead pages, pages that never load: you pay for none of them.

Cached answers say so

Repeat calls inside five minutes are re-served fast. They come back marked cached, with the age in seconds and the timestamp of the original render — never the time of your call. A five-minute-old price sold as live would be a lie.

Nothing is invented

Image URLs are copied from the page, never constructed. A field the page doesn’t state comes back null rather than guessed. Where the merchant publishes schema.org data, that is what you get — real SKUs, real GTINs, real variant lists.

Proof you can check without us

An attestation is worth what it is worth to someone who does not trust us — the other side of a dispute, an auditor. So verifying one is free, needs no account, and holds no database: re-hash the claim, check the signature against a published address, done. The maths is public and anyone can redo it by hand. An attestation naming a different signer was not issued here, whatever it claims.

Built for developers

Nothing proprietary between you and the endpoint. These are plain HTTP APIs on an open protocol — no SDK to adopt, no client library you are stuck with, no account to close. Every tool publishes a machine-readable spec you can implement against directly: OpenAPI, an llms.txt written for agents, and an x402 manifest. Try before you pay: a real extraction, free. And an attestation can be verified against a published signing address by anyone, at no cost, without us.
